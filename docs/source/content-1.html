Chapter 1: Project Proposal

1.1 Introduction

Woredas are the local administrative units through which Ethiopian residents obtain everyday public services such as birth and marriage certificates, residence identification, tax clearance and land holding certificates. Each service is delivered by a department of the woreda administration at a physical desk, and residents must appear in person to be served.

This project, WoredaCare: Woreda Booking and Appointment Management System, is a web-based system that lets citizens book these in-person visits in advance, lets each department review the requests and serve citizens through an organized desk queue, and gives the woreda administration central control over departments, services, working schedules, staff accounts and an audit trail of every appointment decision.

1.1.1 Existing System

The system is designed to replace an in-person, walk-in process in which citizens arrive at the woreda office, wait in a physical queue on a first-come, first-served basis, and learn only at the desk whether the required documents are complete or whether the service is available that day. There is no way to reserve a time, no visibility of the expected waiting time, and decisions about requests are not recorded centrally.

1.2 Motivation

The motivation for the project comes from the cost that the walk-in process places on both citizens and staff:

  • Citizens lose working time travelling to the office and waiting without knowing when, or whether, they will be served.
  • Departments cannot plan their daily workload because demand is unknown until citizens arrive.
  • Holidays and office closures are communicated poorly, so citizens may travel on days when no service is available.
  • Decisions such as approving, rejecting or rescheduling a request leave no reliable record that the administration can review.

Widespread access to mobile phones and web browsers makes it practical to move the booking step online while keeping the service itself at the desk.

1.3 Problem Statement

Woreda public services are delivered through unscheduled walk-in queues. As a result, citizens experience long and unpredictable waiting times, departments cannot control their daily capacity, citizens are not reliably informed about non-working days, and there is no transparent, auditable record of who decided what about each request. The problem this project addresses is the absence of a system that allows citizens to reserve a specific service time, allows departments to review and serve those reservations in an orderly queue, and gives the administration control and visibility over schedules and decisions.

1.4 Objectives

1.4.1 General Objective

To design and develop a web-based booking and appointment management system that enables citizens to schedule woreda public services online and enables woreda departments and administrators to manage, serve and audit those appointments.

1.4.2 Specific Objectives

  • To allow citizens to register with an Ethiopian mobile number and book an appointment for a specific service, date and time.
  • To enforce working hours, lunch breaks, daily capacities and calendar exceptions (holidays, non-working days, blocked dates) when bookings are made.
  • To let department officers approve, reject, reschedule and cancel requests, check citizens in at the desk, call them to a counter and complete the service.
  • To show citizens their live queue position, the number of citizens ahead and behind them, and a countdown to their appointment time.
  • To give administrators management of staff accounts, departments, services, operating hours and calendar exceptions.
  • To record every appointment decision in an audit log with before and after snapshots.
  • To protect the system with authentication, role-based access control and protection against common web attacks.

1.7 Application

The system is useful to three groups:

  • Citizens can book a visit from a phone or computer, avoid closed days, see whether their request has been approved, follow their place in the queue and cancel when they cannot attend.
  • Department officers know their daily demand in advance, decide requests in one place, and run the desk queue with numbered tokens and counters.
  • The woreda administration configures services and schedules centrally, declares holidays once for all departments, and reviews an auditable record of every decision.

Chapter 2: Requirement Analysis Document (RAD)

2.1 Introduction

2.1.1 Introduction to the System

WoredaCare is a web application with four kinds of users. Visitors can browse services and register. Citizens book and follow their appointments. Department officers decide requests and run the desk queue for their own department. The administrator manages the configuration of the whole woreda. The system consists of a React single-page client, an Express REST API with a WebSocket channel for live updates, and a PostgreSQL database.

2.1.2 Scope of the System

The system covers the full life of an appointment, from the citizen's request to the completion of the service at the desk, together with the configuration that governs when appointments can be booked. The boundaries of the project are listed in Table 1.1. This document distinguishes features that are implemented, partially implemented and not implemented; the status of each is stated where relevant and summarized in Section 2.3.4.

As described in Section 1.1.1, the current process is a manual walk-in queue. Its weaknesses, which the proposed system addresses, are: no advance reservation, unpredictable waiting, no control of daily capacity, poor communication of closures, and no central record of decisions. No existing software system was replaced by this project; consequently, no existing data was migrated.

2.3 Proposed System

2.3.1 Overview

In the proposed system a citizen books an appointment online. The request starts as Pending and is reviewed by an officer of the responsible department, who approves or rejects it. On the day of the visit the officer checks the citizen in, which issues a numbered queue token; the officer then calls the citizen to a counter and completes the service or records a no-show. Every step is written to the audit log and pushed live to the pages that display it. Figure 2.1 shows the life cycle of an appointment.

IDCategoryRequirement
NFR-01SecurityPasswords are stored as bcrypt hashes and must be at least eight characters with a digit or symbol.
NFR-02SecuritySessions use a signed JWT (HS256) in an HttpOnly cookie (Secure in production, SameSite=Lax). State-changing requests made with the cookie require a matching CSRF token and an allowed Origin.
NFR-03SecurityRole, department, service and ownership rules are enforced on the server for every endpoint; a citizen can never read or change another citizen's data.
NFR-04SecurityAll input is validated (Zod); invalid input is answered with HTTP 422 and field-level messages. All SQL statements are parameterized.
NFR-05SecurityRequests are rate-limited per IP address (5 per second with a burst of 50; sign-in and registration 20 per minute with a burst of 10). Security headers are set with Helmet and request bodies are limited to 1 MB.
NFR-06IntegrityA time slot can never be booked twice and capacities can never be exceeded, even under simultaneous requests; status changes are atomic and conditional.
NFR-07IntegrityAudit entries are only added, never changed or deleted.
NFR-08PerformanceLong lists are paged on the server; the audit log uses keyset pagination so that pages remain fast and stable as it grows.
NFR-09Real timeChanges are pushed to open pages over WebSocket and appear within about a second; the connection reconnects automatically and the queue pages also refresh on a short timer as a fallback.
NFR-10ReliabilityFailure to record an audit entry or to send a live notice never makes the user's action fail; such errors are logged.
NFR-11UsabilityPages work at mobile phone width without horizontal page scrolling; messages are plain language; countdown timers are announced to screen readers as readable text and animation respects reduced-motion preferences.
NFR-12LocalizationAll dates and times are woreda time (Africa/Addis_Ababa, UTC+3) regardless of the device time zone; countdowns use the server clock. Phone numbers follow the format +2519XXXXXXXX.
NFR-13MaintainabilityThe API follows a strict layering (routes, middleware, controllers, services, repositories) with one response envelope; the schema is managed by versioned, reversible migrations; logs are structured JSON with a request identifier.

2.4 System Models

2.4.1 Scenarios

The following scenarios describe typical uses of the system. The names are illustrative.

Scenario 1: Booking a birth certificate. Almaz signs in with her phone number and opens the Appointment page. She taps Book Slot on Birth Certificate Issuance. The form opens on the next working day and shows the free times, with the lunch break unavailable, and her expected order in the queue. She picks 9:00 AM and confirms. The system re-checks the date, validates the request on the server and creates the appointment as Pending with a reference number. The appointment appears as the first row of My Appointments with a dashed countdown marked "awaiting approval".

Scenario 2: Deciding requests and serving at the desk. Hana, an officer of the Civil Registration department, opens her Appointment page and approves Almaz's request; Almaz's countdown becomes solid without reloading. On the day of the visit Almaz arrives, and Hana checks her in, which issues token CRV-02. When the previous citizen has been served, Hana calls the next citizen to counter 2. Almaz's My Queue page shows "It's your turn! Please proceed to Counter Desk #2". After serving her, Hana clicks Complete Service; Almaz's row shows Served and a notice confirms the completion.

Scenario 3: Declaring a public holiday. The administrator opens Calendar Exceptions and adds a public holiday for all departments. From that moment the date is closed for booking: a citizen who has the booking form open on that date sees a six-second notice immediately, and the server refuses any booking for the date.

Scenario 4: Cancelling an approved appointment. Dani has an approved appointment in three days but can no longer attend. On My Queue he clicks Cancel. Because the appointment is more than four hours away, the cancellation is accepted, the time becomes bookable again, the officer's list shows the appointment as Cancelled in red, and the audit log records the cancellation with Dani as the actor.
